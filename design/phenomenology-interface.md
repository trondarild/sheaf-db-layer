Yes: classical Husserlian phenomenology will integrate sparsely if treated as *doctrine* or *transcendental vocabulary*. It integrates much better if shifted into a **typed observational discipline of first-person organization**.

I would re-imagine it this way:

**Do not model “phenomenology” as claims about consciousness. Model it as a structured coordinate system over lived experience.**

So instead of importing terms like *epoché*, *intentionality*, *noesis/noema*, *horizon*, *lifeworld* as philosophical objects, translate them into relational schemas:

`Experience episode → intentional object → bodily orientation → affective tone → temporal structure → salience field → action-readiness → reportability constraints`

Then the functor to bio-neuro layers has something to preserve.

The key move is from **Husserlian phenomenology as transcendental analysis** to **phenomenology as constraint-generating morphology of experience**. That is close to Varela’s neurophenomenology: first-person and third-person accounts are linked through “mutual constraints,” not simple reduction. ([achrafkassioui.com][1]) Gallagher’s “front-loaded phenomenology” is also relevant: phenomenological distinctions can be used upstream in experimental design, rather than being appended after neural data. ([Digital Commons][2])

For your database/sheaf system, I’d separate three phenomenological layers.

First, **raw subjective report**: “I feel pressure in my chest,” “time feels slowed,” “the room feels unreal,” “I feel watched,” “my body feels absent.” This is noisy, idiomatic, culturally variable.

Second, **phenomenological coding**: reports are mapped into dimensions such as embodiment, agency, ownership, temporal flow, affective valence, spatial orientation, intersubjective stance, self-world boundary, object-directedness, and background horizon.

Third, **cross-level anchoring**: each coded dimension links sparsely or densely to candidate mechanisms: interoception, autonomic regulation, predictive precision, dopaminergic salience, inflammation, vestibular integration, social threat, trauma, etc.

The sheaf-theoretic picture might be:

`local subjective patches = situated experiential descriptions`

`overlaps = shared invariants across reports, tasks, disorders, cultures, or bodily states`

`gluing = construction of a stable experiential phenotype`

`obstruction = where first-person, behavioral, neural, or social descriptions fail to cohere`

That last part is valuable. Classical phenomenology may not always glue smoothly to neurobiology. But the **failure to glue** is itself data. For example, depersonalization, chronic pain, panic, psychosis, fatigue, nausea, dissociation, and depression may all involve cases where subjective organization and biomedical categories cut across each other awkwardly.

To optimise functorial faithfulness, I’d avoid asking:

“What neural mechanism corresponds to Husserlian intentionality?”

Ask instead:

“What distinctions in lived experience must be preserved across mappings?”

For example:

`mineness ≠ agency ≠ ownership ≠ control`

`bodily sensation ≠ bodily presence ≠ body image ≠ body schema`

`affect ≠ emotion ≠ mood ≠ valence ≠ arousal`

`social meaning ≠ perceived intention ≠ norm violation ≠ threat`

A faithful functor should not collapse these prematurely.

So the practical redesign is:

**Phenomenology becomes a typed interface layer between subjective reports and mechanistic levels.**

Not:

`Husserl → neuroscience`

but:

`structured lived-experience grammar → empirical anchors → cross-field sheaf`

A useful motto for the implementation:

`Do not naturalize phenomenology by reducing it; naturalize it by making its distinctions operationally preservable.`
